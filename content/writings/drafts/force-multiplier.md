## Building Research Capability

* **Creating research templates and frameworks** - Developing standardized interview guides, survey templates, or usability test protocols that non-researchers can use effectively
* **Running research training workshops** - Teaching product managers, designers, and engineers how to conduct their own user interviews or analyze findings
* **Building a research repository/wiki** - Documenting past research, insights, and methodologies so the team can reference and learn from previous work without re-asking the same questions
* **Establishing research standards** - Creating guidelines for what constitutes valid research, how to recruit participants, how to analyze data—so others do it consistently
* **Mentoring junior designers/PMs in research** - Pairing them with research projects so they develop research skills over time

## The Reinforcing Cycle

**Capability → Capacity:**
* When you build team capability (teach PMs to do research, create templates), you directly increase capacity because more people can do research work
* A team with strong research skills can conduct studies faster and with better quality, multiplying output

**Capacity → Capability:**
* When you increase capacity (automate data collection, build tools), you free up time for researchers to focus on harder problems and skill-building
* More research throughput means more learning opportunities—the team sees more data patterns, gets better at analysis, develops intuition

## The Multiplier Effect

The real power is when they work together:

* **Without capability:** More capacity (hiring more researchers) doesn't help much if they lack skills—you get slower, lower-quality work
* **Without capacity:** Building capability (training the team) has limited impact if people are too busy with existing work to apply new skills
* **With both:** A skilled team with efficient processes can do 3-4x more research than a small team without infrastructure

## Practical Example

| Scenario | Outcome |
|---|---|
| High capability, low capacity | One expert researcher bottlenecked; insights sit in reports unread |
| Low capability, high capacity | Lots of research happening, but poor quality; insights are unreliable |
| High capability + high capacity | Research embedded in decision-making; insights flow continuously; team grows skills through volume |


The best UX people recognize that **capability and capacity are interdependent—you need to invest in both simultaneously, not sequentially.**

They understand:

* **Building only capability without capacity is futile** - You can train your team brilliantly, but if they're drowning in work, they won't have time to apply new skills or do research at all

* **Building only capacity without capability backfires** - Hiring more researchers or automating processes without ensuring quality standards just scales mediocrity

* **The real leverage is in the system, not the individual** - The goal isn't to make yourself indispensable; it's to create conditions where the entire team can do better research faster

* **This requires different types of work** - Some days you're teaching (capability), some days you're building tools (capacity), some days you're removing blockers (enabling both)

* **It's a strategic choice, not accidental** - You have to deliberately ask: "What's limiting us right now—skills or bandwidth?" and invest accordingly

* **Scaling research is about multiplication, not addition** - The best outcome isn't hiring 5 more researchers; it's enabling 20 people across the org to ask better research questions and interpret data themselves

This mindset separates researchers who are just "doing research" from those who are actually **transforming how their organization learns about users.**\
